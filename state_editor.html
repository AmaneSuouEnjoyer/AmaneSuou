What the numbers mean

Issue values are whole numbers from 1 to 10. In the game, a candidate scores more points in a state the closer their own stance is to that state's numbers.

Issue names and questions for this election

The game always has these six issue slots, so scoring and bonuses stay the same. For each election you can rename a slot and change what 1 and 10 mean (for example, rename Immigration to Civil rights for 1968). Leave a box empty to use the default. The wording of Partisanship's scale is fixed, only its name can change.

Senate elections for this election year

Tick the box if this election has Senate races. Fill in the seats that are NOT up for election (independents go under the party they caucus with), then for each state with a race choose how many seats are up (0, 1 or 2) and enter the two candidates. Advantage is 1 to 50 and adds that many points to the candidate in the game. Names are optional. Washington DC has no senators.

Caucuses

Groups such as Southern Democrats. They exist only after you add them here. A caucus has stances (1 to 10) but no popularity, and its stances can only be changed here, not in the game. In a deadlocked presidential election each caucus votes as a block for the candidate closest to its stances. Anyone without a caucus is a "party loyalist" and votes for their party's candidate, so you never have to assign everyone.

Senators not up for election (optional)

List the senators who are not up this year and put them in caucuses. Names are optional. Whoever you do not list is counted as a party loyalist.
